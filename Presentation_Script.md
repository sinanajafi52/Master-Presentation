# Master Thesis Presentation Script
## "Agile Business Structure and Organizational Management for Startups: Designing Adaptive and Scalable Enterprises"

**Goal:** 20 Minutes Presentation | 12 Slides + 3 Backup
**Audience:** Prof. Dr. Denis Royer (First Supervisor), Prof. Dr. Wolfram Ludwig (Second Supervisor), Examination Board
**Key Advice:** Be transparent about methodology, focus on findings, explain the "how" briefly but clearly.

---

## Slide 1: Title Slide (0:00 - 1:00)
**"Good morning everyone. Thank you for being here."**

"I am Sina Najafi. Today I present my master thesis on **'Agile Business Structure and Organizational Management for Startups'** — designing adaptive and scalable enterprises.

My research focuses on a critical moment in every startup's life: when the team is small — 2 to 10 people — resources are tight, and the pressure to grow is high. Specifically, I looked at how these micro-enterprises stay flexible while also adding the structure they need — within the German institutional context."

---

## Slide 2: The Problem & Research Gap (1:00 - 2:30)
**"Let's start with why this matters."**

"**74.6% of German startups have fewer than 10 employees** — according to the Startup-Verband 2024 report. Yet virtually all existing research on organizational agility focuses on large enterprises with 50 or more employees.

This means most German startups have no evidence-based guidance. Founders face critical decisions:
- *When should they formalize processes?*
- *Which agile practices actually work in micro-enterprises?*
- *How do they stay flexible while introducing structure?*

And in the German context — with Geschäftsführer personal liability, tax documentation requirements, and sector-specific regulations — founders cannot simply copy Silicon Valley playbooks.

The research gap is clear: no study had looked at organizational agility specifically in 2-to-10-person ventures within the German institutional environment."

---

## Slide 3: Research Questions (2:30 - 3:30)
**"This led me to my main research question:"**

**"How can early-stage German startups with 2 to 10 employees design agile business structures that enable organizational adaptability, scalability, and long-term sustainability?"**

"To answer this, I broke it into four sub-questions:
1. **What is** organizational agility in early-stage startups — and how does it differ from software agile?
2. **What factors** — structural, cultural, and leadership — enable or constrain it?
3. **How can startups maintain** agility while scaling — and what obstacles emerge?
4. **What design principles** can founders use to build adaptive and sustainable structures?"

---

## Slide 4: Theoretical Framework (3:30 - 5:00)
**"To build the foundation, I used three theoretical lenses."**

"First — **Dynamic Capabilities Theory**, Teece 1997. It explains **WHAT** agility looks like: sensing changes, seizing opportunities, and transforming resources.

Second — **Complexity Leadership Theory**, Uhl-Bien 2007. It explains **HOW** leadership enables agility — not by commanding, but by enabling the team to self-organize. Three functions: adaptive, enabling, and administrative.

Third — **Contingency Theory**, Lawrence and Lorsch 1967. It explains **WHY** there is no one-size-fits-all — because context always matters.

*(Point to Venn diagram)*
My contribution: combining all three lenses specifically for micro-enterprises — for the first time."

---

## Slide 5: Methodology (5:00 - 7:00)
**"Here is how I conducted the research."**

"I used a **qualitative multi-case study** design — four steps:

**Step 1 — Sampling:** **Maximum Variation Sampling** — I selected 7 German startups, Cases A to G, from very different sectors: railway, water, consulting, software, firefighting, and medical AI.

**Step 2 — Data:** Semi-structured founder interviews — **445 minutes** in total.

**Step 3 — Analysis:** **Reflexive Thematic Analysis**, Braun and Clarke 2006. I coded manually — 87 codes, 12 categories.

**Step 4 — Synthesis:** I profiled each case individually, then built a **cross-case comparison matrix** across all seven.

This approach captured nuances that a survey simply could not."

---

## Slide 6: Finding #1 — Five Universal Success Factors (7:00 - 8:30)
**"First finding: five factors that appeared in all seven cases."**

"**One — Trust and Psychological Safety.** Every founder said this was the foundation. Without it, no framework works.

**Two — Learning Orientation.** Six cases used retrospectives. The seventh used rapid iterations.

**Three — Servant and Delegative Leadership.** All seven founders focused on enabling their teams — not commanding them.

**Four — Results over Process.** Outcomes always came before strict ceremony adherence.

**Five — Selective Framework Customization.** No startup adopted any framework 100%. They all adapted."

---

## Slide 7: Finding #2 — Team Experience > Framework Choice (8:30 - 10:30)
**"Second finding — and the most important one."**

**"Team experience matters substantially more than framework choice."**

"Two cases show this clearly:

**Case C** — no formal framework. But the founder had 10 years of agile consulting experience. The mindset was in the team's DNA. Result: **high agility.**

**Case F** — implemented full Scrum. Dailies, sprint planning, retrospectives — everything by the book. But the team was new to agile thinking. They followed the process without understanding the principles behind it. Result: only **medium agility.**

The pattern is consistent across all seven cases.

The message: **invest in agile mindset — not methodology shopping.**"

---

## Slide 8: Finding #3 — Critical 6-8 Person Threshold (10:30 - 12:30)
**"Third finding: the 6 to 8 person threshold."**

"There is a clear tipping point in how teams coordinate:

**Below 6 people** — Cases B, E, G — informal communication works. Everyone knows everything naturally.

**Above 6 to 8 people** — Cases A, C, D, F — communication links explode. At 4 people: 6 links. At 8 people: 28 links. Nearly **five times more** — from just doubling the team.

This is a sharp inflection point. Informal coordination breaks down fast. Case A experienced this directly — even though they were proactive.

**The takeaway:** Put coordination structures in place **before** you reach 6 to 8 people. Not after."

---

## Slide 9: The Five-Layer Conceptual Framework (12:30 - 14:30)
**"Based on these findings, I built the Five-Layer Integrated Framework."**

"This is the core theoretical contribution:

**Layer 1 — Contextual Factors:** Start here. Stage, sector, size, funding, German regulations.

**Layer 2 — Cultural Foundations:** Trust, psychological safety, learning orientation. This layer must come **first**. It is universal.

**Layer 3 — Structural Mechanisms:** Role design, decision distribution, coordination. These come **after** culture. They depend on context.

**Layer 4 — Leadership Approaches:** Empowerment, autonomy, alignment.

**Layer 5 — Agility Outcomes:** Speed, flexibility, responsiveness, sustainable growth.

The core message: most startups jump straight to Layer 3 — Scrum, OKRs — without Layer 2. Structure without culture gives you compliance, not agility."

---

## Slide 10: Six Guiding Principles for Founders (14:30 - 16:30)
**"Six evidence-based principles founders can act on."**

"**One — Foundation First:** Build trust and psychological safety before anything else.

**Two — Gradual Formalization:** Add structure step by step as the team grows. The 6-8 threshold is your signal.

**Three — Principle Extraction:** Don't copy Spotify or Google. Understand *why* their methods work — then adapt the principles to your context.

**Four — Accept Hybridity:** In Germany — with Geschäftsführer liability and sector regulations — pure agile is often not possible. Hybrid models are a necessity, not a failure.

**Five — Monitor the Threshold:** Act at 6 to 8 people. Before, not after.

**Six — Preserve Culture During Growth:** Agility often peaks early and then declines — Case A showed this clearly. Keeping it requires active, deliberate reinforcement."

---

## Slide 11: Limitations & Future Research (16:30 - 17:30)
**"Four honest limitations."**

"**One — Sample size:** 7 cases. Deep patterns, no statistical generalization. Maximum Variation Sampling strengthens transferability.

**Two — Geography:** Germany only. The institutional context limits direct transfer — though cultural foundations are likely universal.

**Three — Cross-sectional design:** A snapshot, not a movie. Longitudinal studies would strengthen this.

**Four — Founder-only perspective:** Team members may see agility differently.

For future research: longitudinal studies, quantitative validation of the experience-framework gap, and extension to other European contexts."

---

## Slide 12: Contributions & Conclusion (17:30 - 20:00)
**"To conclude."**

"**Theoretically:** This is the first study on organizational agility in 2 to 10 person enterprises. First integration of Dynamic Capabilities Theory, Complexity Leadership Theory, and Contingency Theory at this scale. Empirical evidence for the 6-8 person threshold.

**Practically:** A five-layer framework and six principles that German founders can use directly.

The central message of this thesis:

***Organizational agility in micro-enterprises is cultural — not structural. Foundations before frameworks. Context always matters.***

Three takeaways:
1. **Culture before framework.**
2. **Act at 6 to 8 people.**
3. **Design for your context** — not for methodological purity.

**Thank you. I welcome your questions.**"
