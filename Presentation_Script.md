# Master Thesis Presentation Script - "Organizational Agility in German Micro-Enterprises"

**Goal:** 20 Minutes Presentation | 7-14 Slides
**Audience:** Prof. Dr. Denis Royer (Main focus), Examination Board
**Key Advice:** Be transparent about methodology, focus on findings, explain the "how" briefly but clearly.

---

## Slide 1: Title Slide (0:00 - 1:00)
**"Good morning everyone. Thank you for being here."**

"I am [Your Name], and today I will present the findings of my master thesis on **'Organizational Agility in German Micro-Enterprises'**.

My research focuses on a critical phase in a startup's life: the very beginning, when the team is small (2 to 10 people), resources are scarce, and the pressure to grow is immense. Specifically, I looked at how these micro-enterprises navigate the tension between remaining flexible and introducing necessary structure within the German ecosystem."

---

## Slide 2: The Problem (1:00 - 2:30)
**"Let's start with why this matters."**

"We know that **74.6% of German startups fail** because of friction within the founding team, not just product failure.

The core problem is a paradox:
1.  **Too much structure early on kills speed** and innovation (bureaucracy).
2.  **Too little structure leads to chaos** as soon as the team grows beyond a few people.

Founders often struggle with this 'Agility Paradox'. They ask: *'Should we adopt Scrum? Should we use OKRs? Or should we just wing it?'*
Currently, most agile frameworks like SAFe or LeSS are designed for large enterprises, leaving micro-startups without clear guidance."

---

## Slide 3: Research Questions (2:30 - 3:30)
**"This led me to my main research question:"**

**"How can German micro-enterprises (2-10 employees) balance structural formalization with cultural flexibility to sustain organizational agility during their initial growth phase?"**

"To answer this, I broke it down into sub-questions focusing on:
1.  **Contextual constraints** (like German regulations).
2.  **Enabling mechanisms** (Leadership & Culture).
3.  **Structural tipping points** (When does informal coordination break down?)."

---

## Slide 4: Theoretical Framework (3:30 - 5:00)
**"To ground this research, I integrated three complementary theoretical lenses."**

1.  **Dynamic Capabilities Theory:** Explains **HOW** agility happens—through sensing changes, seizing opportunities, and transforming resources.
2.  **Complexity Leadership Theory:** Explains **WHO** drives it—moving away from top-down control to enabling adaptive spaces where the team can self-organize.
3.  **Contingency Theory:** Explains **WHY** there is no 'one-size-fits-all'. It tells us that the right structure depends on context—specifically Stage, Sector, and Size."

*(Point to the center of the Venn diagram)*
"My contribution lies in the synthesis of these three: viewing agility not just as a process, but as a system dependent on leadership and context."

---

## Slide 5: Methodology (5:00 - 7:30)
**"Now, let me briefly explain how I conducted this research."**

*(Walk through the Process Flow at the bottom)*

1.  **Sampling:** "I used **Maximum Variation Sampling** to select **7 distinct cases** (Cases A to G). They range from Deep-tech and Railway Infrastructure to Consulting and Medical Education AI. This diversity ensures that the findings are robust across different industries."
2.  **Data Collection:** "I conducted in-depth, semi-structured interviews with founders, totaling **445 minutes** of audio data."
3.  **Analysis:** "I used **Reflexive Thematic Analysis** (Braun & Clarke). I manually coded the transcripts—creating a detailed coding table which you can find in the appendix—to ensure I stayed close to the data."
4.  **Synthesis:** "I first profiled each case individually and then created a **Cross-Case Matrix** to compare patterns across all startups."

"This rigorous qualitative approach allowed me to identify the subtle cultural and structural nuances that quantitative surveys might miss."

---

## Slide 6: Success Factors (7:30 - 9:00)
**"Moving on to the findings. I identified five universal success factors across all successful cases."**

"The most critical one is **Trust & Psychological Safety**. Without this foundation, no method works.
Also crucial is **Selective Framework Customization**. Successful founders didn't just 'do Scrum'; they picked parts of frameworks that solved their specific problems. They extracted principles rather than adopting rigid rules."

---

## Slide 7: Key Finding #1 - Experience vs. Framework (9:00 - 11:00)
**"This is perhaps the most significant discovery of my thesis."**

**"Team Experience matters substantially more than Framework Choice."**

"Let's look at **Case C (Consulting)** and **Case F (Software)**.
*   **Case C** had *no formal framework* but a high-agility outcome. Why? because the founder had 10+ years of agile consulting experience. The mindset was embedded in the team.
*   **Case F** implemented *Full Scrum*—dailies, retrospectives, everything. Yet, their agility was only 'Medium'. They were doing the rituals without the agile mindset.

The evidence suggests that prior agile experience contributes **significantly** more—possibly by an order of magnitude—to agility outcomes than simply choosing 'Scrum' or 'Kanban'."

---

## Slide 8: Key Finding #2 - The Threshold (11:00 - 13:00)
**"Another critical finding is the '6-8 Person Threshold'."**

"I observed a distinct tipping point.
*   **Below 6 people:** Informal communication works perfectly. Everyone knows everything.
*   **Above 8 people:** Communication lines explode (following the n(n-1)/2 rule). Informal coordination fails, leading to chaos.

Case F ignored this and faced a crisis. Case A anticipated it and introduced lightweight structure just in time.
**The Takeaway:** Founders must proactively introduce 'Gradual Formalization' *before* hitting this 8-person wall."

---

## Slide 9: The Five-Layer Framework (13:00 - 15:00)
**"Based on these findings, I developed the 'Five-Layer Integrated Framework'."**

"This is the core theoretical contribution. It hierarchizes the elements of agility:
1.  **Layer 1 (Context):** You must start with your constraints (Size, Sector).
2.  **Layer 2 (Culture):** This is the **Prerequisite**. You generally cannot build agility without Trust and Safety.
3.  **Layers 3 & 4 (Structure & Leadership):** Only *after* culture is set should you introduce structural mechanisms and leadership styles.

Many startups fail because they start at Layer 3 (structures) without establishing Layer 2 (culture)."

---

## Slide 10: Guiding Principles (15:00 - 17:00)
**"Translating this into practice for founders, I propose Six Guiding Principles."**

1.  **Foundation First:** Build trust before processes.
2.  **Gradual Formalization:** Don't over-structure early; add rules only when friction appears.
3.  **Principle Extraction:** Don't copy Spotify or Google. Understand *why* they do it, and adapt the principle to your context.
4.  **Accept Hybridity:** Especially in Germany, with our regulatory environment, 'pure' agile is often impossible. Hybrid models are a pragmatic necessity, not a failure.

---

## Slide 11: Limitations (17:00 - 18:00)
**"Transparency is key to research. There are limitations."**

"First, this is a **Qualitative Study** with 7 cases. While it offers depth, broad generalization (e.g., to all German startups) would require large-scale quantitative testing.
Second, the **Single-Informant Bias**: I interviewed founders. Their perception of agility might differ from their employees'.
Future research should include longitudinal studies to see how these dynamics evolve over time."

---

## Slide 12: Conclusion (18:00 - 20:00)
**"To conclude:"**

"Organizational agility in micro-enterprises is **fundamentally cultural, not just structural**.
*   It requires **Foundations before Frameworks**.
*   It is heavily dependent on the **Context** (Stage & Sector).
*   And experienced teams outperform teams that simply follow a rulebook.

I believe this framework provides a practical roadmap for German founders to build resilient, adaptive organizations from day one.

**Thank you for your attention. I welcome your questions.**"
